This study compares interpretable and nonlinear classifiers for identifying existing gallstone status from demographic, laboratory, and bioimpedance measurements. A secondary analysis of 319 records with 38 candidate predictors evaluated logistic regression, random forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, and stacking using leave-one-patient-out internal validation, implemented as record-level leave-one-out under a one-record-per-person assumption. Training-fitted preparation retained 20 predictors through recursive CART-based selection. Fixed configurations were compared with nested Optuna tuning using 20 trials and three inner folds per outer training set. Tuned logistic regression achieved the highest pooled ROC--AUC (0.868) and F1 (0.796), while matching XGBoost's sensitivity (0.826). LightGBM matched logistic regression's accuracy (0.787), and LightGBM and stacking shared the highest specificity (0.759). XGBoost showed the largest descriptive improvement over its historical fixed benchmark, whereas tuning did not consistently improve the other families. SHAP and LIME illustrated model reliance and confident classification errors. Historical dependency differences, prior cohort exploration, retrospective family comparison, and absent external validation limit inference. The findings concern existing disease status and do not establish prospective prediction or clinical utility.
