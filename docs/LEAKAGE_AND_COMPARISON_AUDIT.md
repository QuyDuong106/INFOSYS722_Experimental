# Leakage, comparison and workflow audit

**Current audit:** [The completed five-model dynamic-evaluation audit](audit/CURRENT_EVALUATION_AUDIT.md) supersedes the historical fixed-k findings below. It verifies all current checkpoints/trials and all 1,276 saved individual pipeline predictions, while retaining unresolved design and provenance limitations.

Reviewed 22 October 2026. Scope: the five configurations in the current research paper, their saved predictions/checkpoints/trials, current training and explanation code, historical version-1 training source at commit `bc917c0`, and the main legacy notebook entry points. This is a code/evidence audit, not a fresh full retraining or a certification of the clinical source data. The manuscript and trained results were not changed.

**Update, 23 October 2026:** The four individual models have since completed dynamic feature-count tuning with the same k candidates as Stacking. Finding 1 below describes the earlier fixed-k20 comparison and is resolved for the updated paper's candidate-count restriction. Search-budget differences, retrospective selection and source-data limitations remain. Current metrics and feature summaries are generated by `scripts/update_dynamic_results.py`; the numerical comparison below remains a record of the 22 October audit.

## Assessment

**No direct held-out-record leakage was identified in the reported fitting path. The principal confirmed comparison issue is unequal feature-count search spaces. Retrospective development and selection on the same cohort also limit confirmatory interpretation.** There are additional reproducibility problems in legacy entry points. Patient identity and predictor availability at the intended clinical decision time remain unverified.

## Findings, ordered by priority

### 1. High: unequal feature-count search spaces

The four reported individual models use version-1 runs with exactly 20 selected predictors. Stacking uses version 3 and searches 10, 20, 30 or 38. Its selected counts are 1, 45, 34 and 239 outer exclusions, respectively. Thus it uses all 38 predictors in 74.9% of exclusions, while the individual models cannot choose that configuration.

Evidence: `results/optuna_{logistic,random_forest,xgboost,lightgbm}/run_config.json`; `results/stacking_v3/run_config.json`; all 1,595 saved outer checkpoints; `optuna_lopo.py:83` and `optuna_lopo.py:91`. See `docs/audit/saved_run_checks.json` for per-model evidence.

Implication: comparing the observed pipelines is valid as an exploratory comparison of those configurations. It does not isolate an advantage due to Stacking architecture, because feature-count flexibility also changes. The paper already notes the differing search spaces, but disclosure alone does not make the experiment controlled.

Correction: rerun all candidates with the same feature-count candidates, preprocessing implementation, inner/outer splits, seed schedule and environment. Also report an all-model fixed-k comparison if the aim is to isolate architecture from feature-count flexibility. Use new result directories; preserve the current observed evidence.

### 2. High: retrospective selection and development on the same cohort

The training functions tune each family separately and produce one outer prediction per record. They do not select the best family inside each outer training partition. Choosing the family with the highest pooled outer AUC and then reporting that same score as an unbiased estimate of the selected procedure adds a selection step outside evaluation. Earlier exploration and architecture revisions informed by this cohort can add further optimism. These are research-design concerns even when every individual fit excludes its test record.

Evidence: `optuna_lopo.py:209`–`230` evaluates all requested families separately; current Stacking and the individual models have different search versions. Manuscript discussion acknowledges prior exploration and retrospective choice. The exact extent of historical decision-making cannot be reconstructed from checkpoints alone, so the magnitude of optimism is unknown.

Correction: describe the present results as exploratory internal comparisons. Freeze the entire design before an independent external evaluation. If estimating a family-selection procedure by resampling, select the family and all development choices using outer-training data only. A new split of an already explored cohort does not restore an untouched external assessment.

Methodological source: [Varma and Simon, 2006](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2105-7-91).

### 3. Medium: equal trial counts do not establish equal tuning effort

All candidates have 20 completed trials per outer fold, but the search dimensions differ:

| Candidate | Searched dimensions | Feature-count choices |
|---|---:|---|
| Logistic regression | 1 | 20 only |
| Random forest | 4 | 20 only |
| XGBoost | 6 | 20 only |
| LightGBM | 6 | 20 only |
| Stacking | 14 | 10, 20, 30, 38 |

Stacking also requires substantially more estimator fits because its base outputs are cross-fitted. Equal trials neither guarantee equal optimisation difficulty nor equal compute. Larger spaces can be underexplored; more compute can also help. The observed evidence does not establish the direction or magnitude of this effect. Twenty trials are not necessarily twenty distinct parameter configurations.

Correction: justify the selected budget, report measured runtime/fit counts and, if feasible, examine sensitivity to a prespecified larger budget. Distinguish a common-trial-budget comparison from a common-compute comparison. Include Ridge as an individual comparator and a two-base ablation if claiming that combining the three components provides additional value. Their absence does not invalidate the current score, but leaves the source of any ensemble benefit unresolved.

Evidence: `optuna_lopo.py:50`–`96`; saved trial parameter columns. Fit times in the audit JSON are recorded run times, not controlled cross-machine compute benchmarks.

### 4. Medium: a fresh legacy notebook workflow has an obsolete preprocessing lookup

`notebooks/transformation_feature_engineering.ipynb`, cell index 7 (zero-based), requests `numeric.named_steps["standardize"]`. The current factory creates only `PowerTransformer(..., standardize=True)` inside the numeric pipeline (`utils.py:162`–`165`). A fresh current preparation therefore raises `KeyError('standardize')` at that notebook step. This mismatch was reproduced using a fitted current preparation. It can be masked by an older cached transformer containing the old separate scaler.

`run_workflow.py:8` includes this notebook before model stages. The full default run is consequently not a reliable clean reproduction path. The paper's current saved evaluation reads raw data directly and does not depend on this visualisation cell; no evidence connects this bug to contamination of the reported predictions.

Correction: update the notebook to handle the current combined power transformation/standardisation and rerun it from clean exports. Do not interpret this failure as proof that the evaluated models leaked data.

### 5. Medium: cache and workflow provenance need strengthening

`utils.py:245`–`251` reuses exported preparation when dataset/split/configuration fields match, without comparing preprocessing code or dependency versions. `optuna_lopo.py:186`–`199` checks considerably more metadata, including package versions and a manually maintained search version, but does not bind checkpoints to a code hash. Changing implementation without changing recorded configuration can therefore reuse stale exports or mix fold implementations on resume. The audit found no proof that such mixing occurred in the current saved runs.

The current default runner uses version-3 feature-count search for all new models, while the paper's four individual candidates are version-1 evidence. Running today's defaults will not reproduce those exact historical configurations. The runner's legacy figure stages also do not constitute a single complete rebuild of the current CART, Stacking XAI and manuscript artifacts.

Correction: add a source/configuration fingerprint to caches and checkpoints; use explicit run manifests recording the code commit and uncommitted source hash; define one documented, versioned paper workflow. Retain separate directories for experimental and paper evidence.

### 6. Unresolved: patient identity and clinical predictor availability

No exact duplicate predictor rows were found, but source-row IDs are not verified patient IDs. Distinct rows can still belong to the same person. If that occurs, record-level exclusion can leave related records in training. Likewise, the repository does not establish that every predictor is available before the intended clinical decision, independently of the diagnostic process or subsequent care.

These are unresolved source-data questions, not confirmed instances of leakage. Confirm one record per person or use grouped exclusion. Obtain a predictor-availability/provenance table for the intended use case, including measurement time and acquisition procedure. Existing-status classification should not be presented as prospective disease-onset prediction.

## Checks that passed

- Raw loading excludes the outcome and the exported row-index column. The analysis contains 319 rows, 38 predictors and 161 disease-positive labels, with no missing predictor cells or exact duplicate predictor rows. All shared named clinical values in the CSV and original XLSX agree exactly.
- All five reported result folders have the same dataset signature, package versions, seed, threshold and outcome encoding. All five use the same outer exclusions and the observed sampler schedule `42 + fold`.
- All 1,595 checkpoints align with the saved labels and probabilities. All 31,900 recorded trials are marked complete. Each model has exactly one prediction for every source row. Stored decisions use threshold 0.5, and recomputed AUC, accuracy, precision, sensitivity and specificity match saved metrics.
- `utils.py:154`–`170` places imputation, supervised RFE and transformations inside the estimator pipeline. `optuna_lopo.py:132`–`150` fits fresh clones within training folds before predicting validation records.
- Stacking contains complete base pipelines and uses cross-validated base outputs for the logistic meta-model (`optuna_lopo.py:38`; `modeling.py:39`). It does not use `cv='prefit'` or train the meta-model on in-sample full-base predictions. This matches the intended [scikit-learn StackingClassifier design](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.StackingClassifier.html).
- A one-trial synthetic boundary audit instrumented the actual RFE fits inside Stacking: 48 fits in total. The outer excluded ID appeared in none. Each tuning-fold fit excluded its validation records; each block contained three full-training base fits and nine smaller base-cross-fitting fits. This is direct evidence about the current implementation, not a retrospective trace of every original training operation. Evidence: `docs/audit/stacking_fit_boundaries.json`.
- The eight existing tests pass, including training-partition isolation, resumability, target encoding, raw-input persistence and real Stacking configuration checks.
- The paper's SHAP/LIME script reconstructs the excluded-row model using its saved parameters, uses training-only backgrounds and checks probability agreement (`scripts/build_xai_report.py:35`–`56`). CART summaries reconstruct training-only selectors. These summaries are descriptive outputs and are not fed into the evaluated training loop.

The reviewed fitting organisation follows the standard recommendation to fit learned preprocessing on training partitions only: [scikit-learn leakage guidance](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/common_pitfalls.html#data-leakage).

## Interpretation of the observed difference

Stacking AUC is 0.87617 versus logistic regression's 0.86795: a descriptive difference of **0.00822**. At threshold 0.5, Stacking classifies six additional records correctly overall: it corrects 24 logistic-regression errors but misses 18 records that logistic regression classifies correctly. These counts do not establish statistical superiority, especially under different feature-count searches and retrospective comparison.

A defensible statement is: “Stacking achieved the highest observed discrimination among the evaluated configurations. Its feature-count search differed from the individual classifiers, and prior dataset exploration and retrospective comparison limit causal and confirmatory interpretation.”

## Recommended order of work

1. Repair the notebook/pipeline mismatch and bind future caches to source versions.
2. Freeze a comparison specification; rerun all candidates with matched feature-count choices and consistent development boundaries.
3. Add Ridge and suitable ablations if interpreting an ensemble-specific benefit; prespecify budget and seed sensitivity checks.
4. Preserve the full training-only evaluation boundary even if the manuscript omits the optimiser's name or a dedicated tuning heading.
5. Treat the current ranking as exploratory and pursue an untouched external, appropriately grouped clinical cohort for confirmatory assessment.

Existing results should remain unchanged until new experiments are actually completed. No retraining, result replacement or manuscript revision was performed as part of this audit.
